Phishing & Credential Compromise — NexSecure SOC Report

📅 March 2026 · NexSecure Bootcamp 👤 Nathaniel T.O

🔍 Detection Logic

Detection began via a SIEM alert triggered by an unusual spike in failed authentication events against a corporate email gateway. The alert fired at 02:14 UTC — outside normal business hours — correlating with a phishing email campaign targeting 23 employee accounts. Initial triage revealed a lookalike domain spoofing the company's internal IT helpdesk.

⚠ Initial Alert

SIEM Rule: auth_failure_spike — 47 failed logins within 8 minutes across 12 accounts from IP range 185.220.x.x (TOR exit node range).

🧠 Analyst Reasoning

The attack chain followed a well-documented Business Email Compromise (BEC) pattern: targeted spear-phishing with a credential harvesting page hosted on a compromised legitimate domain to bypass URL reputation filters. The attacker's use of a TOR exit node for credential testing indicates operational awareness — they expected the brute-force phase to be detected and used anonymising infrastructure accordingly.

The time of attack (02:14 UTC) was deliberate — targeting a window when SOC monitoring is typically at reduced capacity in many organisations.

🛡️ Mitigation Strategy

  • Enable MFA on all email accounts — credential theft becomes useless without the second factor
  • Deploy email authentication (SPF, DKIM, DMARC) to prevent domain spoofing
  • Implement anti-phishing URL scanning with sandbox detonation for unknown domains
  • Conduct regular phishing simulation training — users who click should receive immediate targeted training
  • Create a 24/7 SOC monitoring schedule — off-hours attacks are deliberate
